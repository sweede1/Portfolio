## Executive Summary

This executive summary outlines the proposed revisions to Georgia State University's (GSU) parking policy to make it more inclusive for students residing in campus-adjacent areas. As the university continues to grow and student housing remains limited, many students find themselves living just outside the campus perimeter. It is essential for ensuring all students regardless of housing status can fully access campus resources and opportunities. 

By revising the current policy, GSU can improve student experience, strengthen community ties, and align more closely with institutional goals.

### Key Points:
- These students, who often live within walking or biking distance but still require occasional or flexible vehicle access, are not adequately considered in the existing system
- This disconnect between the needs of a growing off-campus population and the university's infrastructure inhibits inclusivity and reduces student satisfaction
- Benefits include overall improvement in student satisfaction and cohesion with the student body, community, and university goals

### Expected Outcomes:
Students will enjoy greater convenience and access, particularly those who live near campus but currently face logistical challenges. Revising Georgia State University's parking policy to be more inclusive of campus-adjacent students is a critical step toward fostering an equitable and supportive educational environment.

## Background

### University Context
Georgia State University (GSU) has a large student population offering over 250 majors and minors across degree levels across multiple campuses. The demand for convenient and accessible parking solutions for students, particularly those living close to campus, has increased significantly.

**Current Statistics:**
- **Total student population across 6 GSU campuses:** 52,000 students
- **On-campus housing occupancy:** Only 19% of students live in college-owned, operated, or affiliated housing
- **Housing challenge:** Due to high demand for student housing versus limited supply, many GSU students have no choice but to seek alternative options to remain close to campus

### Historical Context
GSU has made substantial investments in parking infrastructure, including development of multiple parking decks and lots. However, with rising enrollment and growing numbers of students choosing to live near campus, there is a pressing need to re-evaluate and enhance existing parking services.

### Current Gap
The introduction of additional parking options such as:
- Overnight parking availability
- Designated lots for campus-adjacent students
- Semester parking passes for off-campus residents

...would significantly improve adjacent students' access to affordable parking, alleviate daily stress from parking searches, reduce parking violations, and contribute to a safer campus environment.

## Problem Statement

### Core Issues

#### 1. No Parking Options for Campus-Adjacent Students
Currently, there are **no options** for campus-adjacent students to park in GSU-provided parking facilities. Existing policies only consider:
- GSU students living on campus
- Students who commute to and from campus

Campus-adjacent students have been completely overlooked.

#### 2. Increased Cost of Attendance
Due to the above problems, the cost of parking for campus-adjacent GSU students' cost of attendance has increased significantly.

**Associated impacts:**
- Increased stress on adjacent students
- Adverse effects on student academic performance
- Potential decrease in GSU student rankings and school rankings

#### 3. Underutilized Infrastructure & Lost Revenue
- School parking facilities sit empty after closing hours
- Facilities don't generate revenue during downtime while still incurring costs
- **Missed opportunity:** Campus-adjacent students would utilize campus-provided lots as an alternative option for parking

#### 4. Reduced Student Flexibility & Opportunity
Some students opt not to purchase or keep a car on/close to campus because the total cost is too high due to parking expenses. This:
- Reduces student flexibility
- Limits viable job opportunities
- Negatively impacts student career prospects

### Specific Policy Gaps

#### Overnight Parking is Not Available
- GSU parking policy **strictly prohibits** overnight parking in lots allocated for commuting and campus-adjacent students
- Warning: Cars left overnight will be towed
- Students cannot study in groups after hours or access campus facilities during extended hours

**Survey Finding:** Pie Chart 1.1 shows more surveyed students would like overnight parking implemented than those who don't care about it.

#### 24-Hour Lots are for Campus Residents Only
Campus lots designated as "24hr lots" include:
- Lofts parking Deck
- Patton Hall lots and decks
- Commons parking
- Piedmont North and Central dorms parking

**Restriction:** Registration requires proof of residency in these specific dorms through the Housing portal, making them inaccessible to campus-adjacent students.

#### Parking Cost Disparity for Adjacent Students

| Category | Cost per Semester |
|----------|------------------|
| On-Campus Students | $400 |
| Campus-Adjacent Students | $600–$800 |
| **Difference** | **$200–$400 MORE** |

**Monthly breakdown for adjacent students:** $150–$200/month vs. on-campus students' $400/semester

This significant disparity is illustrated in Bar Graph 1.2, showing that parking is a major cost burden for off-campus students.

## Proposed Solution

### Recommended Approach
The most feasible solutions are to:

1. **Extend overnight parking accommodations** to include options in campus-provided structures
2. **Create semester parking options** for campus-adjacent students in designated lots (preferably closer to dorms that don't reach full capacity during the day)

### Implementation Strategy
**If implemented, the parking policy would:**
- Be inclusive of all student types
- Serve university services better
- Reduce cost of attendance for interested students by lowering parking costs

### Additional Recommendation
To further reduce budget constraints' impact, negotiate partnerships with nearby residential buildings where GSU students already live:
- **For property owners:** Boost occupancy rates and increase revenue
- **For students:** Reduce cost of attendance
- **For GSU:** Improve student outlook and perception of university value

### Benefits of Partnership Model
- Win-win scenario for all parties
- Demonstrates university commitment to student welfare
- Creates mutually beneficial community relationships
